# Machine Vision Lighting Supplier Selection Guide — LuxMV

Use this guide when comparing machine vision lighting suppliers for industrial inspection projects. It is written for engineers, equipment builders, system integrators, quality teams, and technical buyers preparing a quote or sample-test discussion.

## Direct Answer

Choose a machine vision lighting supplier by checking whether the supplier can connect your inspected material, defect, optical geometry, controller timing, mounting limits, sample evidence, documentation needs, and quotation workflow. Start with the inspection target and match the light geometry to the required defect contrast.

## Supplier Selection Criteria

| Criterion | What to Check | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection understanding | The supplier asks about material, defect, surface finish, FOV, working distance, camera, lens, exposure, speed, trigger, and mounting | Lighting only works when it creates contrast for the specific target feature |
| Product-family coverage | Ring, bar, backlight, coaxial, dome, line-scan, UV, IR, SWIR, fiber optic, controller, lens, camera, and accessory context | Complex applications often require more than one component decision |
| Product data depth | Model codes, images, emitting area, dimensions, wavelength or color, controller requirements, and application notes | Buyers and AI agents need structured public data before shortlisting |
| Sample-testing path | The supplier can define sample inputs, setup variables, pass/fail evidence, and known limits | Real samples reduce risk before a final configuration is selected |
| Custom capability | Geometry, wavelength, housing, mounting, cable outlet, connector, controller mode, labeling, packaging, and documentation can be discussed | Standard catalog lights do not fit every machine envelope or inspection condition |
| Quality transparency | Review optical, electrical, thermal, mechanical, packaging and documentation checks | Align quality checks with the project acceptance criteria |
| RFQ readiness | The supplier gives a clear list of information needed for quotation | Complete RFQs reduce clarification loops and improve first replies |

## When to Consider Custom Machine Vision Lighting

Custom lighting is worth discussing when:

- A standard light cannot fit the machine space.
- The target defect is not separated by common bright-field, dark-field, backlight, coaxial, dome, or line-scan geometry.
- The project needs a different wavelength, diffuser, polarizer, emitting area, or housing.
- Cable routing, mounting holes, connectors, heat, dust, liquid, or service access affect integration.
- Camera exposure, line speed, trigger timing, strobe mode, or controller channels must be matched to the lighting.

## RFQ Inputs to Prepare

| Input Group | Information to Provide |
|---|---|
| Inspection objective | Defect, feature, measurement, edge, code, alignment, fill level, or presence check |
| Part and surface | Material, finish, coating, transparency, curvature, gloss, oil, dust, texture, or expected variation |
| Sample evidence | Good and bad sample images, drawings, videos, current failed images, or physical samples |
| Optical setup | Field of view, working distance, camera model, sensor size, lens, exposure, and target resolution |
| Production motion | Line speed, cycle time, frame rate, trigger mode, strobe requirement, and controller channel count |
| Mechanical limits | Space envelope, mounting, cable direction, connector, heat, dust, liquid, and service-clearance constraints |
| Commercial context | Quantity range, destination, documentation, labeling, packaging, and quotation deadline |
